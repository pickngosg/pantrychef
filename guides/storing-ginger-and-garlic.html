How to store ginger and garlic

By PantryChef SG · Updated 1 November 2026

Short answer: Store whole garlic bulbs in a cool, dry, airy place, not the fridge. Keep ginger wrapped in paper in the fridge, or freeze it. Never keep homemade garlic in oil at room temperature.

Garlic

Whole bulbs: keep in a cool, dry, ventilated spot away from sunlight, such as a basket. They last weeks.

Peeled cloves: keep in a sealed container in the fridge and use within a few days.

Chopped or minced garlic: freeze in small portions, for example in an ice cube tray with a little water.

Garlic in oil is a food safety risk. Garlic stored in oil at room temperature can grow the bacteria that cause botulism. If you make it, keep it in the fridge and use it quickly, or freeze it.

Ginger

Unpeeled ginger: wrap in a paper towel, put in a bag, and keep in the fridge chiller. It keeps for a few weeks.

For longer storage, freeze whole pieces. Frozen ginger is easy to grate straight from the freezer.

If ginger gets mouldy, throw that piece away.

Frequently asked questions

Should garlic go in the fridge?

Whole bulbs do better in a cool, dry, airy place. The fridge can make them sprout or turn rubbery.

Can I freeze ginger?

Yes. Freeze it whole or grated in small portions. The texture softens but the flavour stays.

Is sprouting garlic safe?

It is usually safe but may taste bitter. Remove the green shoot if you want a milder flavour.
