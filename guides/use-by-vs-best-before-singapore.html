"Use by" vs "best before" in Singapore

By PantryChef SG · Updated 1 November 2026

Short answer: "Use by" is a safety date for highly perishable food: do not eat it after that date. "Best before" is about quality: the food may be less tasty after the date but is not automatically unsafe. Shops in Singapore cannot sell food past either date.

Use by

Used on highly perishable food such as fresh meat, fish and some dairy. The Singapore Food Agency says you should not eat these after the use by date, even if they look and smell fine.

Best before

Used on longer-lasting food such as rice, canned goods, biscuits and sauces. It shows when the maker expects the food to be at its best quality. After the date, the taste, texture or colour may fade. Check storage conditions, packaging and signs of spoilage before deciding.

What shops must do

Shops in Singapore are not allowed to sell food after either date. If you spot expired items on shelves, you can report it to the shop or the Singapore Food Agency.

How to use this at home

Check the date before cooking. Plan "use by" items first.

Store food as the label says, such as "keep refrigerated".

Once opened, many items have a shorter life than the printed date.

Frequently asked questions

Can I eat food after the best before date?

It may still be fine if it was stored properly and looks and smells normal, but quality may be lower. Throw it away if you see any signs of spoilage.

Can I eat food after the use by date?

No. Use by dates are for safety. Throw the food away.

Which date applies once I open the pack?

Many foods have a shorter safe life once opened. Follow any on-pack instructions such as "use within 3 days of opening".
